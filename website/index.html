A data science project

ADHD diagnosis prediction using machine learning

I wanted to find out how far machine learning can go in predicting an ADHD diagnosis. Using the public ADHD-200 dataset (935 children from nine clinics), I cleaned and explored the data, compared 13 algorithms, looked inside the models to understand their decisions, and tested whether brain scans add anything.

01 · The project

What I set out to do

Why ADHD?

ADHD affects roughly 5–7 % of children. Diagnosing it takes interviews and questionnaires, which is slow, and clinicians don't always agree. I wanted to know whether a model could pick up the same patterns from simpler data, and to be honest about where it can't.

My goals

  • Combine messy data from ten clinics into one clean table
  • Understand how the groups differ before modelling
  • Compare many models fairly
  • Explain why the models decide what they do
  • See if brain connectivity helps

Two questions

Set A Can ADHD be predicted from age, sex and IQ alone, with no questionnaire?

Set B How well does a screening model work once parents' symptom ratings are added?

What I built

  • 6 Jupyter notebooks, run from start to finish
  • A small Python package, src/neuroadhd
  • Trained models you can reload (.joblib)
  • 39 figures and result files
  • This website

02 · The data

The data I worked with

The ADHD-200 Consortium shared clinical records and brain scans from children and teenagers at clinics in the USA, China and the Netherlands. The catch is that every clinic recorded things a little differently, so most of the early work was getting them to agree.

How I cleaned it

Missing values-999 and N/A treated as missing
Duplicates101 repeated rows removed
Diagnosistypically developing → 0, any ADHD type → 1
Handednessrecorded the same way at every clinic (right / left)
Questionnairestwo different rating scales put on one 0–1 scale
IQfull 4-part score, or the 2-part score if that's all there was
Set asideBrown's 26 children (no diagnoses released)

03 · Exploring the data

How do children with ADHD differ?

Before training anything, I wanted to see what actually separates children with ADHD from typically developing children, and what might mislead a model. Four things stood out.

Sex

Boys were diagnosed about twice as often as girls (49 % vs 22 %).

IQ

Children with ADHD scored a little lower on every IQ test, but the two groups overlap a lot.

Symptoms

Parents' ratings separate the groups almost perfectly, because they're part of how ADHD is diagnosed.

Clinic

Which clinic a child went to is strongly linked to their diagnosis. That's a warning sign for any model.

04 · Results

How well did the models do?

I compared 13 algorithms against a simple "always guess the most common answer" baseline, using 80 % of the children. I tuned the four best, tried combining them, and only then tested on the remaining 20 %, once. That final score is the one I trust.

Would it work at a new clinic?

The clinic problem

Logistic regression on Set A, cross-validated AUC:

Given only the clinic's name, a model already scores 0.70. Adding it would make the numbers look better without helping a new clinic at all, so I left it out.

Is it fair to girls?

Without a questionnaire, the model catches far fewer ADHD cases in girls. It learned that "boy" is a strong clue, which mirrors a real bias in how ADHD gets diagnosed.

05 · Explainability

Why does the model decide what it decides?

A score alone doesn't tell you why a model makes a decision, and in anything medical the why matters. So I looked inside the models with permutation importance and SHAP. It turned out to be the most interesting part of the project.

06 · Brain connectivity

Can brain scans tell the difference?

For this part I used resting-state fMRI scans from 40 children. I split each brain into regions, measured how strongly each pair of regions works together, and gave those connections to the models. I tried two brain maps and three ways of measuring connectivity, repeated every test many times, and checked the best result against pure luck.

Best result for each model

My take: 40 scans just aren't enough. With hundreds of connections per child and so few children, the models can't find a reliable pattern.
