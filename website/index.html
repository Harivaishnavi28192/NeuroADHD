01 · Research Scope

What this study set out to answer

ADHD affects 5–7% of school-age children worldwide. Traditional clinical evaluation is resource-intensive, relies on subjective parent-teacher interviews, and exhibits significant inter-clinician variance. This benchmark investigates whether machine learning can reliably assist screening and rigorously inspects potential clinical pitfalls.

Clinical Motivation

Current diagnosis requires comprehensive semi-structured interviews and neuropsychological testing. We examine whether non-invasive demographic and cognitive indicators carry genuine signal, or if diagnostic questionnaires remain irreplaceable.

Core Objectives

  • Harmonise heterogeneous clinical data across 10 centers
  • Screen for confounding site bias and demographic shifts
  • Rigorous 5-fold cross-validation across 13 algorithms
  • Explain black-box decisions with SHAP & PDP
  • Quantify predictive utility of resting-state fMRI

Two Clinical Questions

Set A Can ADHD be identified from demographics (age, sex) and IQ alone without questionnaire administration?

Set B What diagnostic ceiling is reached when adding standardised parent symptom ratings?

02 · The ADHD-200 Dataset

Multicenter clinical cohort & preprocessing

The ADHD-200 Consortium pooled phenotypic records and neuroimaging from clinics across the United States, China, and the Netherlands. Because each site operated independent intake protocols and assessment scales, robust data harmonisation was required.

Preprocessing Protocol

Missing CodesMapped sentinel codes (-999, N/A) to NaN
Duplicate ScrubIdentified and excised 101 repeated participants
Target BinarisationTypically developing (0) vs ADHD Any Subtype (1)
Handedness StandardHarmonised disparate site encodings into dominant hand
Questionnaire UnificationMapped Conners' T-scores and ADHD-RS to 0–1 continuous severity
Cognitive ScalingFull 4-subtest IQ prioritised, fallback to 2-subtest WISC
Unlabelled HoldoutBrown site (n=26) quarantined for zero-shot testing

03 · Exploratory Data Analysis

Biomarker discovery & statistical findings

Before training machine learning algorithms, we conducted hypothesis testing (Student's t, Mann-Whitney U, χ² tests), calculated effect sizes (Cohen's d), and inspected dimensionality projections to isolate genuine signal from noise.

Sex Disparity 2.2×

Boys were diagnosed at more than double the rate of girls (49% vs 22%), reflecting widespread referral and diagnostic asymmetries in child psychiatry.

Cognitive Shift d ≈ 0.4

Children with ADHD scored modestly lower across verbal, performance, and full IQ, but inter-group distributions exhibit overwhelming overlap.

Symptom Severity d > 2.0

Parent rating questionnaires separate groups almost completely (Cohen's d > 2.0), because symptom counts directly constitute the DSM diagnosis criteria.

Site Confounder AUC 0.70

A classifier trained solely on clinic identity achieves 0.70 AUC without looking at the patient, mandating Leave-One-Site-Out (LOSO) validation.

04 · Model Zoo & Benchmark

Comparative evaluation of 13 algorithms

We benchmarked 13 algorithms against a majority baseline using nested 5-fold cross-validation on 80% of participants. Hyperparameters were tuned via RandomizedSearchCV, ensembles (Voting and Stacking) were constructed, and final generalization was evaluated once on the locked 20% holdout with 1,000 bootstrap replicates.

Site Confounder Audit

Logistic Regression on Set A (cross-validated AUC):

A classifier provided only the clinic's ID name scores 0.70 AUC. Including clinic ID artificially inflates metrics while guaranteeing deployment failure at new clinics, so it was strictly excluded from production models.

Gender Fairness Disparity

Critical ethical finding: Without symptom surveys, the demographic model achieves 75% sensitivity for boys but drops to 25% for girls. The model codified historical clinical under-referral patterns.

05 · Explainable AI (XAI)

Inside the black box: SHAP & feature mechanics

In clinical healthcare, high predictive accuracy is insufficient without interpretability. We deployed TreeSHAP and KernelSHAP to decompose individual predictions into additive feature contributions, leading to the discovery and resolution of a major data artifact.

Case Study: Uncovering the NYU Handedness Artifact with SHAP
During initial model exploration, handedness unexpectedly ranked as the 2nd most predictive feature in Set A. SHAP force plots revealed that left-handedness was heavily associated with ADHD. Further investigation revealed NYU (where 58% of children had ADHD) encoded handedness as a continuous Edinburgh Inventory score, while other sites recorded simple binary flags. Once harmonised to true dominant hand, handedness importance collapsed to near-zero, averting a spurious clinical claim.

06 · Resting-State fMRI Connectome

Can resting-state brain networks predict ADHD?

Using preprocessed resting-state fMRI scans from 40 subjects via Nilearn, we extracted blood-oxygen-level-dependent (BOLD) time series across two brain parcellation schemes (MSDL and BASC-64) and calculated functional connectivity matrices via covariance, correlation, and tangent space embedding.

Top Connectome Configurations

Scientific Verdict: In small sample cohorts (n=40), the curse of dimensionality (2,016 connection features vs 40 subjects) precludes robust machine learning generalization. Permutation testing proves the modest 0.63 AUC is not statistically distinguishable from chance.
